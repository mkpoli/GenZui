# GenZui Serif 0.107

Five forms have revised outlines: TOMO, both NARI forms, SHITE and the hooked
WU alternate. Curved WU remains the default; `ss01` selects Hooked WU.

| Form | Stroke treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Hooked WU | Each side of the bowed stem is one continuous cubic through the main descent. Its tangent aligns with the short lower return. |
| TOMO | MO’s left entry is shorter and lower, giving TO’s nearby head more space. The MO shaft and lower return retain their 0.106 geometry. |
| Katakana NARI | The lower stroke has a taller shoulder and more weight through its entry, neck, descent and bowl. The upper SHI stroke is unchanged. |
| Hiragana NARI | The upper stroke is unchanged. The low wave has a fuller shoulder, steadier descending weight and the translated lower finish of Noto え. |
| SHITE | The dot retains its length and direct join. Scaling along its axis is 114%; transverse scaling is 104%, bringing its weight closer to the NO-derived stroke. |

TOMO appears between TOKI and TOTE in both writing directions. Its reference
panel includes Noto に, ほ and モ. In に and ほ, the adjacent stroke entries
have greater horizontal separation and different entry heights. TOMO uses a
smaller local adjustment to its MO entry while preserving the gap beside TO.

Hiragana NARI’s reference panel shows え and へ. The wave ends with a full,
rounded kana terminal. The native upper diagonal and foot remain in place.
Katakana NARI retains the same separate upper stroke and has substantially more
ink in the lower stroke than 0.106.

Accepted KOTO, TOKI, TOTE and YORI remain unchanged. The gallery compares the
five revised forms with 0.106; unchanged forms appear once. Every reading sample
has horizontal and vertical versions.

The printed NARI sources remain available under each card’s references:
[操觚便覧, 1899](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/863176/1/12) and
[和漢の文典, 1902](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/864212/1/36).
Their proportions inform the character structure. Stroke weight and terminals
follow the surrounding Noto kana.
