02 / OKINAWAN INPUT沖縄語の文字入力

Write in Okinawan.

Compose with Funatsu’s New Okinawan Kana or the prefecture’s raised katakana. Copy the result into an app that uses GenZui.

Copied text may be unreadable elsewhere.

Some letters use Unicode’s Private Use Area (PUA). Copied PUA text does not work reliably in other apps, websites or messages: it may appear as boxes or unrelated symbols. You and your recipients need GenZui or a font with matching PUA mappings enabled.

Type kana with your usual keyboard, or select Romaji and type lowercase syllables. Select a letter below to insert its shortcut at the cursor. Nothing is sent to a server.

In kana mode, とぅ・てぃ・くゎ・すぃ become Funatsu letters. In romaji mode use tu ti kwa si tsi; tsu chi shi give ordinary つ・ち・し. Use du di gwa zi dzi for their voiced forms. yi ye produce い゙・え゙.

{'ya}, {'yu}, {'yo}, {'wa}, {'wi}, {'we} and {'n} select the glottal initial forms. Funatsu uses these at the start of a word. Plain ya yu yo wa wi we n remain ordinary kana.

Use ^a ^i ^u ^e ^o ^tsu ^fu ^n to insert the eight raised letters. A raised vowel before a full vowel marks a non-glottal onset: ^uウトゥ (husband) contrasts with ウトゥ (sound). Raised ツ marks the glottal onset in the guide’s specified contexts; raised フ distinguishes /f/ from /ɸ/. Choose these signs according to the variety you are writing.

The guide covers several Ryukyuan languages. This keyboard provides its eight raised signs; it does not infer pronunciation or convert between orthographies.

In romaji mode, n' separates a nasal from the next vowel (kan'i → かんい); double a consonant for small っ, or type xtsu. Use - for ー. Uppercase text and punctuation stay as typed. Check the result before copying.

Ordinary kana and combining dakuten use Unicode; the remaining forms use documented PUA conventions. Raised signs are designed for horizontal text. Download the mappings.

Encoding & reference charts

The palette contains all 27 forms in Funatsu’s chart and all eight raised katakana in the prefectural proposal. Voiced Funatsu forms use a PUA base followed by U+3099. YI and YE use U+3044/U+3048 followed by U+3099. The input tool also converts the older Nishiki-teki PUA equivalents to these sequences.

Funatsu bases follow Nishiki-teki’s U+F450–U+F46A convention (18 selected positions). Raised katakana follow Xim Sans: U+E532–U+E537, U+E4EF and U+E5AA. These assignments are private agreements. Proposal code points are not used; U+1B168 remains SMALL ARCHAIC YE.

  • Okinawa Prefecture’s guide (PDF pages 11–12 and 26) · Raised-katakana proposal
  • Nishiki-teki PUA chart · Xim Sans mapping documentation

GenZui uses its own Noto-based drawings. Toranekoman’s kana fonts, FROkinawaMoji and しま書体 were consulted as implementation references; their glyph outlines are not included.
